All right, our final stretch, Section 5, Combinatorial Auctions and VCG.
Let's go back to that radio spectrum example. Telecom companies don't usually want just one

They use something called XR bids. That stands for exclusive OR. So a telecom company might

auctions so incredibly complicated. To tackle this massive headache,
economists came up with the Vickery Clark Groves, or VCG mechanism. It basically takes the enforced
honesty of the Vickery auction and scales it all the way up to this insane combinatorial level.
Here's how it works. In a VCG auction, each agent pays the cost to the other agents of having
participated in the auction at all. Think of it like this. You are basically paying for the
harm your winning causes everyone else by taking those specific items off the board.
Why is this such a game changer? Well, first off, it manages to solve that immense combinatorial
complexity. If you were to simplify VCG down to just a single item, the math completely

Vickery auction. But even better, it manages to untangle even the craziest XR bid puzzles

absolutely zero benefit for an agent to declare anything other than their true valuation,
